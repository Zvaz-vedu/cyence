    title: "Genetics started here"
    text: |
      In the mid-19th century, **Gregor Johann Mendel** carried out his famous pea-crossing experiments in the garden of the Augustinian Abbey in Old Brno. By carefully observing thousands of plants, he uncovered the basic principles of heredity and laid the foundations of modern genetics.

      He presented his groundbreaking results in Brno in 1865, but the scientific world only recognised their true significance decades later. Today Mendel is considered the father of genetics, and the place of his discoveries is commemorated by the Mendel Museum of Masaryk University.

      Brno continues this scientific tradition to this day. The city where Mendel studied the heredity of plants has become a major centre of molecular biology, genetics, medicine and other fields.

      Brno is one of the world's centres of **electron microscopy** – roughly a third of the world's electron microscopes are made here. These cutting-edge instruments let scientists examine the world in extraordinary detail, from the structure of materials to individual cells, and research institutions all over the world use them.

      But local research reaches much further. Brno's universities, research centres and technology companies take part in developing space technology, artificial intelligence, robotics and modern medical procedures. Brno connects a long scientific tradition with innovations that help shape the future.

Brno is the second-largest city in the Czech Republic, a major university centre and one of the leading hubs of research and innovation in Central Europe. A city with a rich scientific history, today it brings together top universities, research institutions and technology companies from all over the world.
